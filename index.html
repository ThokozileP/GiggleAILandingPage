01 — The Problem

Healthcare AI is moving faster than its control systems.

Clinical AI products are moving from static decision-support tools to agentic systems that act inside clinical workflows.

They recommend, triage signals, trigger alerts, escalate cases, defer decisions, override workflow assumptions, and interact with clinicians while care is in motion.

The risk is no longer only whether the model is accurate. The risk is whether the agent behaves safely, predictably, and reconstructably when it is running in production.

Governance stops at approval

Model approval, documentation, and static risk classification do not show what the clinical agent did after deployment.

Runtime behaviour is under-specified

Teams know the intended workflow, but not every path the agent takes when confidence shifts, thresholds fire, context changes, or a clinician intervenes.

Oversight is hard to prove

Logs may capture outputs, but clinical AI oversight requires evidence of what was shown, who acted, what changed, and why the system escalated or deferred.

Incidents become reconstruction exercises

Without runtime control, audit, post-market monitoring, MDR AI software evidence, and EU AI Act healthcare AI reviews depend on stitched-together data.

02 — Positioning

Runtime control is
the missing layer.

Healthcare AI companies need to govern behaviour, not just documentation. Clinical agent runtime control makes agent behaviour visible, owned, interruptible, and reconstructable while the product is operating in real workflows.

The control layer sits between model approval and operational evidence. It shows when the system acted, why it acted, what context it used, and who intervened.

06 — Regulatory Alignment

Runtime oversight for regulated healthcare AI.

Clinical agent runtime control supports the evidence healthcare AI companies need when oversight, intervention, auditability, and post-market monitoring move from policy language into deployed product behaviour.

EU AI Act
Article 14

Effective Human Oversight

Human oversight AI evidence must show more than a named reviewer. Runtime control defines when a clinician can intervene, what they saw, how the agent behaved, and whether oversight was operational at the point of use.

MDR
GSPR 14.2(d)

Intervention and Override Evidence

MDR AI software needs clear evidence of user intervention, escalation, and override paths where clinical decisions are influenced by automated processes. Runtime oversight design makes those paths explicit and reconstructable.

It is a focused assessment of whether your healthcare AI company can monitor, explain, govern, and reconstruct how clinical agents behave in production workflows. It looks at recommendations, escalations, deferrals, overrides, clinician interventions, runtime monitoring, and audit evidence.
